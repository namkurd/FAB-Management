          How this is scored
          What counts as a miss
          In a completed regular-season week, a starter scored below replacement for his position, and a bench player eligible for that same slot (e.g. a WR for a FLEX spot, a QB for SUPER_FLEX) scored above replacement for his position and more than the starter. A bench player can fix only one slot per week; the biggest holes are filled first with the best eligible option. Simply having good players on the bench never counts against you, and neither does a start that was defensible going in (below).
          Points left on the bench
          Bench player's points − starter's points for each miss. Per week divides by regular-season weeks played, so seasons (and the partial current one) compare fairly.
          Cost a win
          A week you lost where the misses that week add up to more than the margin — the right call would have won it.
          Rumbles lost
          The league's standings: each week, 9 Rumbles for winning your matchup plus 1 for every team you outscored. Rumbles lost = what you'd have earned with that week's right calls (your score + points left on the bench, everyone else's score unchanged) minus what you actually earned. It counts both the flipped matchup and the teams you'd have passed.
          Judging the call (projections)
          Hindsight is easy, so every call is checked against Sleeper's pre-game projections for that week, scored with this league's settings, and sorted into a 2 × 2:

          Replacement level
          The same per-season, per-position table as the Pickups page and the Trade Network (see that page's method section).
          Limits
          Sleeper doesn't record when a player sat on IR, so an IR-stashed player who returned and scored can show up as a bench option. Late scratches (a starter ruled out after his lineup locked) count as misses.
